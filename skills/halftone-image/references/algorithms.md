# Halftoning algorithms in this Skill

Output is binary. Methods fall in three families (Ulichney-style taxonomy); the viewer and `scripts/halftone.py` implement the same code.

| Family | Methods here | Character | Use for |
|---|---|---|---|
| **AM screen** (clustered dots/lines) | `am`: grids square / hex / radial / ring; shapes round, ellipse, square, diamond, line, cross, ring; cell size, angle, cell-centre sampling, tone curve, dot gain, noise warp | Print look; dot area = darkness; robust at coarse scale | Bold 3D textures; posters; engraving look |
| **Ordered / threshold-array** | `bayer` 2/4/8/16, `blue` (void-and-cluster 64x64), `ign` (interleaved gradient noise), `white` | Fast, repeatable, no error carry; Bayer shows a crosshatch pattern, blue noise looks like film grain without clumps | Retro look, large flat areas, stipple |
| **Error diffusion** (FM / stochastic) | Floyd-Steinberg, Jarvis-Judice-Ninke, Stucki, Atkinson, Burkes, Sierra, Sierra Two-row, Sierra Lite; serpentine scan; diffusion amount; `riemersma` (Hilbert-curve error history) | Highest detail per pixel, 1-pixel dots | Fine photos at high resolution; avoid when pixels are smaller than the printable feature |
| **Stipple** | `stipple`: variable-radius Poisson-disk points, dot size follows tone, repulsion relaxation | Hand-drawn dotwork look; dot density and size both carry tone | Portraits, engraving-style art, large smooth areas |
| **Threshold** | `threshold` | Hard edges | Logos, line art, text |

## Notes
- **Error-diffusion kernels** (weights / divisor, current pixel `*`): Floyd-Steinberg `* 7 / 3 5 1 /16`; Jarvis `7 5 / 3 5 7 5 3 / 1 3 5 3 1 /48`; Stucki `8 4 / 2 4 8 4 2 / 1 2 4 2 1 /42`; Burkes `8 4 / 2 4 8 4 2 /32`; Sierra `5 3 / 2 4 5 4 2 / 2 3 2 /32`; Sierra two-row `4 3 / 1 2 3 2 1 /16`; Sierra Lite `2 / 1 1 /4`; Atkinson spreads only 6/8 of the error (`1 1 / 1 1 1 / 1`, divisor 8), which loses extreme tones and gives the crisp, high-contrast Macintosh look. Serpentine scanning removes the diagonal worm artefacts of fixed-direction scans.
- **Blue noise** is generated at load by the void-and-cluster method (Ulichney 1993): Gaussian energy (sigma 1.5) on a torus, tightest-cluster / largest-void swaps, three ranking phases; seeded, so output is deterministic. It is the best-looking ordered method.
- **Riemersma** walks a Hilbert curve and carries a weighted history of the last N errors (weights 1 down to 1/16): no directional bias, cheap, 1-pixel dots.
- **AM screens** use a 64x64 spot-order table per shape, so dot area equals darkness exactly; the cell tone is taken from a box-blurred copy at the cell centre (`centre: true`) for the clean newsprint look, or per pixel for more detail.
- **Linear light** converts tone to linear reflectance before dithering so the share of ink matches perceived brightness; it usually lightens midtones. Off by default for predictability; use Gamma/Contrast to taste.
- **Printing**: parts are coloured by inkjet (HP MJF, about 300 DPI), so the texture has no geometric minimum feature size; choose the pixel count for the look. `offset` (Dot gain) grows or shrinks every dot to compensate for ink spread.

- **Stipple** (halftonemaker's "stippling" idea, our own implementation): points are generated by Bridson-style Poisson-disk sampling with a spacing that shrinks from `spacingMax` (white) to `spacingMin` (black), so density follows tone. Each dot's radius is `dot * spacing * sqrt(1.7 * darkness / pi)` (or constant with "Size dots by tone" off), which makes ink coverage track darkness. `relax` passes push neighbours that are closer than 1.2x their mean spacing apart. Fully seeded and trig-free, so the browser engine and `halftone.py` produce identical pixels.
- **Wave / levels**: `wave` repeats the tone ramp N times (sawtooth restarts, triangle mirrors) for contour-line banding; auto-levels stretches the 0.5%..99.5% tone range; black/white points clip the window.
